Techniques for the Production of Organic Dates

Date of issue: March 2025

--- Page 2 ---
1- Introduction:

Palm plantations in Tunisia amount to about 4.2 million trees, of which 61% are of the “Deglet Nour” variety. Most oases are located in the governorates of Tozeur and Kebili, and to a lesser extent in Gafsa and Gabes.
This document is part of the effort to highlight appropriate and feasible techniques for producing dates according to the organic farming system in Tunisia.

2- Production Techniques:

1-2- Pruning:

The principle of pruning in organic farming does not differ much from that in conventional farming. However, this operation is of great importance in the organic system as it reduces the risk of diseases and pests by eliminating dormant forms of these pests and diseases found on the leaves that are removed during pruning. In addition, all pruning residues (leaves, fronds, fiber, etc.) are crushed and then used as compost.

Photo 1: Pruning and pollination operations

--- Page 3 ---
Pruning is carried out alongside the following operations: pollination, bunch lowering, and harvesting. The pruning operation consists of removing all dry leaves and those infected by pests and diseases. Some green leaves can also be pruned if necessary, provided that 10 leaves remain for each bunch.

2-2: Fertilization:
Dust from extensive or organic animal production, compost, green manure, and natural minerals represent the best fertilizers for an organic oasis. Liquid organic fertilizers that comply with specifications may also be added as a complement to the aforementioned fertilizers.

Photo 2: Use of compost derived from oasis residues

The amount of fertilizer used varies according to the type and composition of the fertilizer itself, the soil, the trees, and the crops associated with the palms (in the lower and middle layers). Generally, an average of 10 tons of compost per hectare every two years is sufficient.

--- Page 4 ---
As for the period of applying dust, compost, or natural minerals, it should be during winter (after harvest). Liquid organic fertilizers, however, are applied through irrigation water whenever the palm tree or the associated trees and crops need it.

3-2: Thinning:
To obtain good and healthy fruits, thinning operations are necessary and can be performed at:
	•	The spikelet level: by cutting the ends of each spikelet or removing spikelets located in the middle of the bunch, or both. This operation is performed during pollination.
	•	The fruit level: by reducing the number of fruits in the bunch when the fruits reach a size that allows estimating the yield.
	•	The bunch level: by cutting some bunches, leaving one bunch for every 10 green leaves.

Note:
Depending on the size of the palm tree and the number of bunches and fruits, the farmer can perform thinning at all the above-mentioned levels, at two levels, or at only one level.

4-2: Pollination:
Pollen is collected according to the varieties from February to April. Pollination should occur during the warmest period of the day since the pollen grain germinates at 18°C or higher. Each palm is pollinated 1 to 3 times as the female flower clusters open gradually.

Pollination consists of placing 3 or 4 male spikelets in each female inflorescence when it opens. Pollination can also be done by spraying pollen onto the inflorescence using a sprayer, and the pollen can be mixed with flour or talc.

--- Page 5 ---
Pollen can be stored at room temperature for up to two months or at 4°C for one year.

5-2: Oasis Cleaning:
Cleaning the oasis is a very important operation in organic farming. It consists of removing dates and leaves fallen to the ground and infected with diseases and pests, as well as parasitic weeds, etc., immediately after harvest. The purpose of this operation is to:
	•	Reduce the severity of diseases and pests.
	•	Reuse oasis residues by turning them into compost.
	•	Facilitate work inside the oasis (plowing, spreading organic matter, etc.).

Photo 3: Oasis residues

6-2: Irrigation:
The palm prefers to have “its head in the sky and its feet in the water.” Therefore, irrigation priority in the oasis is given first to the palm and then to intercropped plants.

--- Page 6 ---
Palm trees are usually irrigated by flooding, and the water quantity depends on soil type, palm variety, intercrops, climate, etc. For example, the “Deglet Nour” variety in the Jerid region of southwestern Tunisia requires between 20,000 and 24,000 cubic meters of water per hectare per year.

The palm is very sensitive to water accumulation; therefore, a drainage system to remove excess water is essential in the oasis.

Organic fertilization plays an important role in water conservation due to its ability to retain moisture --- one gram of compost can retain about two grams of water.

7-2: Pest and Disease Protection:
1-7-2: Major pests:
A/ Date moth: Ectomyelois ceratoniae Zeller

The date moth is a highly harmful pest, not only to dates but also to pomegranate, almond, apricot, and pear.

This pest attacks dates starting from September and continues inside storage facilities, causing up to 20% of annual date losses. There are four generations of this moth per year, the most important being from May to November.

Photo 4: Date moth

--- Page 7 ---
This insect overwinters as a fully grown larva in:
	•	Dates fallen on the ground and unpollinated ones still on the tree.
	•	Pomegranates remaining on trees or fallen to the ground.
	•	Almond fruits remaining on trees or fallen to the ground (almond trees in oases are rare and not a major source of infestation).

Control and protection methods:
Agricultural methods: Collect infected dates and those fallen on the ground or unpollinated and use them for compost or animal feed to reduce pest numbers and eliminate overwintering sites.
Use of nets: Using nets has shown promising results.
Use of approved biological insecticides:
	•	Spinosad 0.24g/l (commercial name Spintor) at 1 to 1.5 liters per hectare has shown good results.
Biological control: Research has shown that using the following parasitoids: Habrobracon hebetor, Trichogramma cacaecae, and Phanerotoma flavitestacea has yielded excellent results in controlling date moth.

Results related to the use of Trichogramma parasitoid in controlling date moth:
Results clearly indicate that this parasitoid occurs naturally in oases but its natural parasitism rate on date moth eggs does not exceed 3%. However, artificial releases increased parasitism to 78.8%, and the infestation rate dropped to 3.5% in treated palms compared to 16.5% in untreated palms.

--- Page 8 ---
Photo 5: Trichogramma parasitoid

To facilitate its use by farmers, we studied its ability to spread vertically, and results showed that it can reach the bunch level with 100% host egg detection, regardless of the release point on the trunk. Parasitism increased with higher release numbers and decreased with greater distance from the bunches.

• Use of sterile males: This method involves producing sterile males of the date moth in the laboratory and releasing them in the oasis to reduce mating and thus reproduction.
• Treatment of dates in storage: To control the date moth in storage facilities, one of the following methods can be used:
	•	Exposing dates to a low temperature (-25°C) for 3 hours eliminates eggs and larvae.
	•	Using carbon dioxide (CO₂) in special storage rooms where a vacuum is created to allow gas penetration into the fruit.

--- Page 9 ---
This operation lasts about 72 hours.
Before storing dates, clean the facilities, remove date residues, and prevent pest infiltration.

B/ Dust mite: Oligonychus afrasiaticus Mc = Paratetranychus afrasiaticus

This mite belongs to the Tetranychidae family and causes severe damage to dates, making them unfit for consumption.

Prevention and control methods:

First preventive intervention:
During winter, clean the oasis by removing dates hanging on the trees or fallen on the ground, infected leaves, weeds, etc., and include these residues in compost.

Second preventive intervention:
Three weeks after pollination (early to mid-May), during fruit set, spray with a sulfur-based liquid product at a rate of 300g/100L of water, ensuring proper mixing and full coverage of the palm, especially young leaves (heart of the palm). Water salinity and acidity should be low. Spray in early morning or evening.

Third preventive intervention:
During the “green” (immature) fruit stage, use the same materials and method as above, focusing on the fruit bunches.

--- Page 10 ---
Curative intervention:
Begin treatment in July when spider webs appear on the fruits (green to yellow stage). Do not use the same active ingredient consecutively; repeat every 2–3 weeks. Treat the entire oasis, cut heavily infested bunches, and include them in compost.

Biological control: Predatory bugs (Punaise) and ladybugs (Coccinelle) are key natural enemies of the mite; therefore, conditions that favor their presence must be maintained by preserving green hedges and windbreaks to sustain biodiversity.

C/ White scale insect:
This insect belongs to the Homoptera family and causes leaf drying and reduced commercial value of dates.

Prevention and control methods:
Agricultural methods: Cleaning the oasis and using residues for compost helps limit the pest’s spread.
Biological control: Several predators and parasitoids naturally attack the white scale, including:

Mites (Hemisarcoptes malus Shimer), Neuroptera (Chrisopa vulgaris), Coleoptera (Cybocephalus palmarum Peyerth, Chilochorus bipustulatus var iranensis, etc.)

D/ Oryctes beetle:
This insect belongs to the Scarabaeidae family.

Prevention and control methods:
Agricultural methods: Cleaning abandoned oases, removing shoots under palms, and plowing the soil.

--- Page 11 ---
Mechanical control: Searching for and killing the beetle manually.
Avoid using shoots originating from infested areas.

3- Conclusion:
Given its adaptation to soil and climate conditions and the traditional practices of oasis farmers, the palm tree is one of the easiest crops to transition to organic farming. This facilitates better valorization of the date sector, especially for export.
